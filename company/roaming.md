# International roaming

All amounts are in Egyptian pounds and include taxes.

## Zones

- Zone One: Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Jordan, Lebanon, Morocco, and Tunisia.
- Zone Two: the United Kingdom, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Greece, the Netherlands, and Turkey.
- Zone Three: the United States, Canada, China, India, Malaysia, Thailand, South Africa, and Australia.

If a country is not on this list, passes may not work there. The assistant should not guess. It should offer to pass the caller to a human.

## Daily passes

- Zone One Day Pass: one hundred twenty pounds. One gigabyte and thirty minutes.
- Zone Two Day Pass: one hundred ninety pounds. One gigabyte and thirty minutes.
- Zone Three Day Pass: two hundred eighty pounds. Half a gigabyte and twenty minutes.

Fine print for daily passes:

- A pass lasts twenty-four hours from the first call or first data use.
- A pass repeats and is charged again on each new day the phone is used abroad, until roaming is turned off.
- Both making and receiving calls use pass minutes.
- Receiving a text message is free. Sending one costs the pay-as-you-go rate.
- Data stops when the pass data runs out, until the next day's pass starts.

## Weekly pass

- Zone One Week Pass: six hundred pounds for seven days. Seven gigabytes and one hundred twenty minutes.
- There is no weekly pass for Zone Two or Zone Three.

## Pay-as-you-go rates without a pass

- Zone One: twenty pounds per minute, fifteen pounds per megabyte, five pounds per text.
- Zone Two: thirty-five pounds per minute, twenty-five pounds per megabyte, seven pounds per text.
- Zone Three: fifty pounds per minute, forty pounds per megabyte, ten pounds per text.

Prepaid lines can make pay-as-you-go calls and send texts, but cannot use pay-as-you-go data. They need a pass for data.

## Ships and planes

Passes do not work on ships or planes.
On a ship or plane network, calls cost ninety pounds per minute, and data costs one hundred pounds per megabyte.
The phone can connect to these networks by itself at sea. Customers should turn roaming off, or use airplane mode, on board.

## Who can use roaming

- Postpaid lines can use all passes and pay-as-you-go rates.
- Flex Platinum includes three free Zone One days each billing cycle.
- Go Smart, Go Plus, and Go Youth can buy passes from their balance. The pass price must be in the balance on each day.
- Go Week and Go Mini cannot use roaming passes. They can use pay-as-you-go calls and texts only, with no data.
- Home internet plans do not roam.
- Boost bundles do not work abroad, and their time keeps running.

## How to turn on roaming

- Roaming is off by default on all lines.
- In the Nabda app, go to roaming and choose a zone.
- Or dial star one five five star seven hash.
- Or ask the assistant, after level two identity checks.
- Turn it on before leaving Egypt. It takes up to one hour.
- If it was not turned on before leaving, use the app over any Wi-Fi network, or call the roaming desk.

## Bill-shock protections

- A welcome text arrives on landing, with the zone and prices.
- A text warning arrives when eighty percent of pass data is used.
- Pay-as-you-go data stops at one hundred pounds a day, unless the customer replies yes to a text to continue.
- Postpaid lines have a roaming limit of one thousand pounds per billing cycle. Roaming stops at this limit.
- Only a human agent can raise this limit.
- Ship and plane charges count toward the limit, but the daily one hundred pound data stop does not apply to them.

## Lost phone abroad

Call the roaming desk at plus two zero, two, five five five zero, oh one seven seven. It can block the line at any time of day.
